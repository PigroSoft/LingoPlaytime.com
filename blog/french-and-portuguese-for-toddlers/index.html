French and Portuguese for toddlers: first words, tricky bits and a home plan

22 October 2026 · 5 minute read

Raising a toddler in French and Portuguese? Here are sixteen first words side by side, what the two languages share, where they trip people up, and a simple plan for home.

It works whichever of the two is your home language, and if one is the language you all live in and the other is the one you're keeping alive, the plan below is the same. Everything here is for the toddler years: words, sounds and routines, with no spelling or school grammar.

What French and Portuguese share

French and Portuguese share nasal vowels, the sh sound for ch (chat, chave) and the zh sound for j (jardin, jardim), so a child who speaks one already makes sounds the other needs.

Words that look alike in the table: banane / banana, livre / livro, arbre / árvore, and fleur / flor.

Where the pair trips people up

The written languages look close, but the spoken ones are tricky in different ways: French has a throat r and many silent endings, while Portuguese has a wider set of nasal vowels and the lh and nh sounds.

Of the 16 words, 4 change gender between French and Portuguese, for example le ballon and a bola, la voiture and o carro, l'arbre and a árvore, and la chaussure and o sapato. The game says each word with its own article, so the child hears the right one every time.

A simple plan at home

Use the sounds the two share as warm-ups: chat and chave, jardin and jardim, begin with the same sounds in both languages.

The research behind the plan is in two of our other posts. How to raise a bilingual child covers how much of each language a child needs and which routines work, and Which language should you speak to your child explains why the language you speak best is usually the right one to use. Children do best when each language has fluent speakers, regular contact and a reason to be used, and it doesn't take a rigid system.

Questions parents ask

Do French and Portuguese share enough sounds to help?

Yes, nasal vowels and the sh and zh sounds appear in both. They are a good reason to expect a child to find the sounds of the second language familiar.

How early can I start with French and Portuguese?

From birth. Babies learn the sounds of the languages they hear in the first year, and the most useful thing is plenty of fluent, warm speech in each language from people the child is attached to.

Will learning French and Portuguese together delay my toddler's speech?

No. A recent study of 604 children found bilingual and monolingual children reach early language milestones at the same age. Count vocabulary across both languages together when you compare, and see our post on late talking for the signs that deserve a look.
