Spanish and French for toddlers: first words, tricky bits and a home plan

22 October 2026 · 5 minute read

Raising a toddler in Spanish and French? Here are sixteen first words side by side, what the two languages share, where they trip people up, and a simple plan for home.

It works whichever of the two is your home language, and if one is the language you all live in and the other is the one you're keeping alive, the plan below is the same. Everything here is for the toddler years: words, sounds and routines, with no spelling or school grammar.

What Spanish and French share

Spanish and French are both Latin's children, and the match is large: la flor and la fleur, el libro and le livre, la luna and la lune. A helpful rule for parents: Spanish words that begin with es- often start with é- in French, so estrella is étoile, escuela is école and espacio is espace.

Words that look alike in the table: luna / lune, árbol / arbre, flor / fleur, and libro / livre.

Where the pair trips people up

Spanish is written as it is said and French is not, so a child who hears Spanish has a much easier path to reading it. French has nasal vowels, a throat r and silent endings; Spanish has the rolled r, ñ and a more even rhythm. Beware largo, which means long in Spanish, while large in French means wide.

Of the 16 words, 5 change gender between Spanish and French, for example el coche and la voiture. The game says each word with its own article, so the child hears the right one every time.

A simple plan at home

Make the es- to é- game a family sport: estrella is étoile, estudiar is étudier. It works for many words and children love a pattern.

The research behind the plan is in two of our other posts. How to raise a bilingual child covers how much of each language a child needs and which routines work, and Which language should you speak to your child explains why the language you speak best is usually the right one to use. Children do best when each language has fluent speakers, regular contact and a reason to be used, and it doesn't take a rigid system.

Questions parents ask

Will Spanish and French get mixed up?

Sometimes, since they are close. Mixing words while learning is normal and not a sign of confusion; the research on language mixing is covered in our myths post. Keeping each language tied to a person or a time of day helps.

How early can I start with Spanish and French?

From birth. Babies learn the sounds of the languages they hear in the first year, and the most useful thing is plenty of fluent, warm speech in each language from people the child is attached to.

Will learning Spanish and French together delay my toddler's speech?

No. A recent study of 604 children found bilingual and monolingual children reach early language milestones at the same age. Count vocabulary across both languages together when you compare, and see our post on late talking for the signs that deserve a look.
