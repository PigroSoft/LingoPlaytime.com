A Gus on the Go alternative when you want every language in one place

28 October 2026 · 4 minute read

Gus on the Go is one of the more honest options in this crowded field: it covers around thirty languages, a lot of it is free, and the app we checked says it has no ads, no in-app purchases and no tracking. The catch for a multilingual home is that each language is its own app, so one place for all your family's languages is where it stops and where a different tool can help.

What Gus on the Go says it is

The Gus on the Go site says its apps are "available now in 30 different languages" (its own meta description says 28, so treat the number as roughly thirty), invites you to "download our language apps for free", and describes learning through a classic story with a silly twist. The App Store listing for the Russian app describes Gus, a friendly owl, travelling the world, with animations and exercises that teach basic vocabulary. It is rated 4+, labelled made for ages 0 to 5, costs $3.99, and carries the developer's statement that it "does NOT contain ads, in-app purchases, links, tracking or sharing functions". The listing we read had only six ratings, so we have no broad picture of how parents find it.

Where one app per language gets awkward

Each language is its own listing, which suits a family with one target language and gets fiddly when you have two or three, because you install, open and keep track of each one separately. Animated lessons also lean on watching, and a meta-analysis of 59 reports finds that children up to six learn about half a standard deviation less from video than from the same material taught face to face, so the grown-up beside the screen counts more than the app does.

What LingoPlaytime does instead

LingoPlaytime is sixteen small games with one job. You enter your child's name, your home language and up to four languages you want them to hear, and every tap says a word in one of those languages in a recorded voice, with the name worked in. It is completely free for now, with no ads and no accounts. Good places to start are At the market, Noah's Ark and Pairs. You set the languages once and every game uses them, so the same words come round in German one day and Spanish the next without installing anything new.

It teaches single words, with no reading, grammar or progress charts, and it is meant to be played next to you so that you say the word too and point at the real thing. It does not replace speaking the language at home, and the plan for that is in how to raise a bilingual child.

Which one to pick

If you want one specific language at a very low price with no ads, Gus on the Go is hard to beat. If you want several languages together, try games that handle all of them in one place, and read the language pair guides to see how your pair behaves. See also our comparison with Lingokids.

Questions parents ask

How many languages does Gus on the Go have?

Its site says around 30, though the page text and its meta description give slightly different numbers. Each language is a separate app.

Does Gus on the Go have ads?

The App Store listing we read for the Russian app says it does not contain ads, in-app purchases, links, tracking or sharing functions.

Is LingoPlaytime free like Gus on the Go?

LingoPlaytime is completely free for now, with no ads and no accounts. All your family's languages are in one place instead of one app each.
