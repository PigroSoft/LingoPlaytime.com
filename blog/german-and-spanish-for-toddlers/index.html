German and Spanish for toddlers: first words, tricky bits and a home plan

22 October 2026 · 5 minute read

Raising a toddler in German and Spanish? Here are sixteen first words side by side, what the two languages share, where they trip people up, and a simple plan for home.

It works whichever of the two is your home language, and if one is the language you all live in and the other is the one you're keeping alive, the plan below is the same. Everything here is for the toddler years: words, sounds and routines, with no spelling or school grammar.

What German and Spanish share

German and Spanish are distant relatives, so very few of the first words match. What they offer instead is contrast in rhythm: German packs consonants together, while Spanish alternates consonant and vowel in a steady beat, which makes the two easy to tell apart by ear.

None of the 16 words are close lookalikes, which makes this a pair where listening matters more than spotting patterns.

Where the pair trips people up

Spanish gender is easier to guess than German gender, since most nouns ending in -o are masculine and most ending in -a are feminine (el libro, la casa), while German der, die and das mostly have to be memorised. The rolled r, the jota (jardín), and the letter ñ are new to German speakers, and German ü and ch are new to Spanish speakers.

Of the 16 words, 11 change gender between German and Spanish, for example das Auto and el coche, die Sonne and el sol, and der Mond and la luna. The sun and the moon swap, which is a good thing to tell older children. The game says each word with its own article, so the child hears the right one every time.

A simple plan at home

Because the two sound so different, they are easy for a child to keep apart. Tie each to a person or a moment, for instance Spanish at the market stall or with one grandparent and German at bedtime, and let the rest of the day follow whatever is natural for your family.

The research behind the plan is in two of our other posts. How to raise a bilingual child covers how much of each language a child needs and which routines work, and Which language should you speak to your child explains why the language you speak best is usually the right one to use. Children do best when each language has fluent speakers, regular contact and a reason to be used, and it doesn't take a rigid system.

Questions parents ask

Does the different grammar gender confuse a child?

Not in a way that matters at this age. Children learn gender from hearing each noun with its article, language by language. It helps to teach the article together with the noun every time.

How early can I start with German and Spanish?

From birth. Babies learn the sounds of the languages they hear in the first year, and the most useful thing is plenty of fluent, warm speech in each language from people the child is attached to.

Will learning German and Spanish together delay my toddler's speech?

No. A recent study of 604 children found bilingual and monolingual children reach early language milestones at the same age. Count vocabulary across both languages together when you compare, and see our post on late talking for the signs that deserve a look.
