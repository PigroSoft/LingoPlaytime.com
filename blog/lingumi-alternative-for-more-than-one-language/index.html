A Lingumi alternative for toddlers growing up with more than one language

28 October 2026 · 4 minute read

Lingumi does one thing and says so plainly: it teaches English to children aged 2 to 5, with a short lesson each day. If English is the language your child needs to pick up, that focus is a strength, and if your household has two or three languages in it, you will want something that does not treat English as the only target.

What Lingumi says it is

The Lingumi page is headed "Playful English learning for kids age 2-5", describes a "magic window" for language learning in the early years, and suggests children "play one new lesson of 10-15 minutes each day". It also says the app can help a child "learn a new language or develop their mother tongue", though English is the only language the page lists. The page we read does not publish prices and sends you to a separate page for them, so we cannot tell you what it costs.

Where a daily lesson fits

A short, planned lesson suits a family that likes a routine and wants to see a child move through a set order of material. It suits a two-year-old less well on a day when the child wants to press everything and move on, and that is our reading rather than anything Lingumi claims. Whatever the format, the research on screens points the same way: in a study of 88 toddlers, children whose parent joined in learned a new word from a video and children watching without that lead didn't, so the daily ten minutes is best spent next to your child, naming things together.

What LingoPlaytime does instead

LingoPlaytime is sixteen small games with one job. You enter your child's name, your home language and up to four languages you want them to hear, and every tap says a word in one of those languages in a recorded voice, with the name worked in. It is completely free for now, with no ads and no accounts. Good places to start are At the market, Noah's Ark and Pairs. Where Lingumi follows a lesson order, our games have no levels, so a toddler can mash every button and still hear a word each time, and an older child gets asked for a particular word.

It teaches single words, with no reading, grammar or progress charts, and it is meant to be played next to you so that you say the word too and point at the real thing. It does not replace speaking the language at home, and the plan for that is in how to raise a bilingual child.

Which one to pick

If the goal is English and you want a guided daily lesson, Lingumi is built for it. If your child is growing up with German, Spanish, French, Italian or Portuguese in the mix, try games that let you choose the languages, and see our language pair guides for how each pairing behaves. We wrote a similar comparison for Lingokids.
